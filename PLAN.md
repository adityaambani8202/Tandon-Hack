Document pipeline (the core of the product)
1.  App uploads PDF or photos over TLS to the backend, which stores them encrypted.
2.  Worker runs OCR (AWS Textract, or Google Document AI, under a BAA) to get text plus page and bounding-box positions.
3.  Worker sends the OCR text to an LLM with a strict JSON schema and instructions to extract only what is written, with the source page and quote for every field. Use a vendor that signs a BAA (OpenAI enterprise/zero-retention, Azure OpenAI, or AWS Bedrock). Do not use a consumer key.
4.  Validator checks the output: drug names against RxNorm, dose and frequency ranges, date sanity, schema compliance. Anything uncertain is marked low-confidence.
5.  Result is saved as a draft plan. Nothing becomes a reminder until a person confirms it.
6.  On confirm, the schedule generator creates dose events and appointments; the app schedules local notifications.
Secrets rule: the OpenAI key you pasted must be revoked. Store the new one only as a server environment variable or in AWS Secrets Manager (OPENAI_API_KEY), never in the app, in git, or in chat.

4. Feature specifications
4.1 Scan and extract
• Intake: camera with edge detection and glare warning, multi-page capture, PDF import, share-sheet import.
• Extract these types: medications (name, strength, dose, route, frequency, with/without food, duration, taper steps, stop date), appointments (date, time, provider, address, prep), diet rules, activity limits (with duration), rehab exercises, red-flag symptoms and the phone number to call.
• Each item stores source page and an exact quote. Unreadable or ambiguous items are flagged, never guessed.
• Handle handwriting, low-quality photos and multi-column layouts; ask for a retake if OCR confidence is low.
4.2 Confirm screen (safety gate)
• One card per item: extracted text, the source image crop with the passage highlighted, and Confirm / Edit / Remove buttons.
• Order by risk: medications first, then red flags, appointments, restrictions.
• Low-confidence fields are highlighted and block "Finish" until reviewed.
• Show a final summary ("6 medications, 3 appointments, 4 warning signs") and require an explicit confirm. A nurse or caregiver can complete it through a shared review link.
• Store who confirmed and when.
4.3 Daily schedule
• Today view: a timeline (Morning, Midday, Evening, Bedtime) with big cards: meds, meals guidance, exercises, appointments.
• Generator expands rules into dated events, including "twice daily for 10 days", "every 8 hours", and tapers such as "10 mg for 3 days, then 5 mg for 3 days".
• Time-zone and travel safe; user can shift wake, meal and sleep times and the schedule adjusts.
4.4 Medication reminders
• Notification text: "Take 1 tablet of Lisinopril 10 mg now. With food." Actions: Taken, Skip, Snooze 10 min.
• Escalation: repeat after 15 and 30 minutes, then optional phone call, then caregiver alert after the grace window.
• Add your own medication (name, dose, times, days, photo of the label) and mark it "not from discharge".
• Log history with weekly adherence percentage; refill reminder when supply count runs low.
• Warn when two entered medications have the same name. Do not give interaction advice; point to the pharmacist.
4.5 Appointment calendar
• Create events in the phone calendar with address, doctor, and prep notes; link back to the app.
• Reminders: 1 day before and 3 hours before, plus prep alerts ("No food after midnight") at the stated time.
• Open map directions; tap to call the office; "Request a ride" shares the event with a caregiver.
4.6 Diet plans
• Tag diet rules from the papers (low sodium, diabetic, soft, clear liquids, heart-healthy), each with start and end dates.
• Generate 3-day sample meal plans, recipes and a grocery list that match the tags. Label everything "Suggestion. Follow your care team's instructions first."
• Phase changes follow the dates in the papers (liquids, then soft, then regular). The app never advances a phase on its own.
• Show allergies and conflicts; let the user say "I don't eat X".
4.7 Check-ins and red-flag alerts
• Daily check-in (under 60 seconds): meds taken, pain 0-10, swelling, fever, and a free-text note. Questions come from the patient's own red-flag list.
• Rule engine (no AI diagnosis): if an answer matches a red flag from the papers, or a wearable value crosses a threshold stated there, show "Your discharge papers say to call your doctor if this happens. Call now" with the doctor's number and emergency number, and notify the caregiver.
• Emergency language (chest pain, trouble breathing) always shows "Call 911" first.
4.8 Wearables
• Connect Apple Health, Health Connect and Fitbit; pull steps, heart rate, sleep, active minutes.
• Show weekly progress ("You walked 20% more this week") against the patient's own baseline, not a clinical target.
• Only use thresholds that appear in the discharge papers or that a clinician sets.
4.9 Caregiver mode
• Invite by SMS or email code; patient approves and chooses permissions (view schedule, view check-ins, receive alerts).
• Caregiver home: today's status for each linked patient, shared calendar, buttons to call, mark "I'll drive", or log a dose on their behalf (marked as logged by caregiver).
• Alerts: missed dose, skipped check-in, red flag. Quiet hours and severity settings.
4.10 Doctor dashboard (web)
• Patient list with status: on track, needs attention, off track (rules are explicit and configurable, e.g. 2+ missed doses in 48 hours = needs attention).
• Patient detail: adherence chart, check-in answers, wearable trends, confirmed plan, and the original document.
• Summary export (PDF) daily or weekly; clinician can add or edit plan items, which the patient must acknowledge.
4.11 Plain language
• Every medical term gets a simple rewrite shown beside the original ("Hypertension: high blood pressure"). Keep the original wording one tap away. The rewrite is generated at extraction time, shown on the confirm screen, and cached.
5. Senior-friendly design and accessibility
• Type: base text 20 pt, headings 28 pt+, follow the system font-size setting up to 200%; no text in images.
• Touch: buttons at least 64 pt tall, 16 pt spacing, one primary action per screen, no gestures required (no swipe-only, no long-press).
• Contrast: WCAG AA minimum (aim for AAA on core screens); never use color alone for meaning; light and dark themes.
• Navigation: three tabs only (Today, Calendar, Help); onboarding in 5 steps or fewer; a "Large mode" toggle on first launch.
• Voice: read-aloud button on every card; spoken reminders; voice entry for check-ins.
• Screen readers: full VoiceOver and TalkBack labels; test with both.
• Errors: plain, kind wording and one clear way forward; never blame the user.
• Setup help: caregiver can set up the account remotely; QR or link login; biometric unlock instead of passwords.
• Languages: build with i18n from day one (English, Spanish first); reading level around grade 6 for all app-written text.
• Usability testing: at least 8 users aged 70+ per phase, tested on mid-range devices.
6. Safety, privacy and regulation
This section is planning guidance, not legal advice. Have a healthcare privacy attorney and a security assessor review it before launch.
Clinical safety
• Human confirmation gate before any reminder; show source page and quote for every item.
• LLM output is constrained to a JSON schema and cross-checked (RxNorm, dose ranges). Anything not clearly in the document is left blank, not inferred.
• Prompt rules: extract only; no advice, no substitutions, no dose changes.
• Build a golden test set of 200+ real-style (de-identified or synthetic) discharge packets and track field-level accuracy before every release. Block releases that regress on medication fields.
• Clinical advisor (nurse or pharmacist) reviews templates, red-flag wording and the meal-plan disclaimers.
• Clear "This app does not replace your care team" language in onboarding and settings.
Privacy and security (HIPAA)
• Sign BAAs with every vendor that touches PHI: cloud host, OCR, LLM, SMS/voice, email, analytics, error tracking. Use only HIPAA-eligible services and configurations (zero data retention, no training on your data).
• Encrypt in transit (TLS 1.2+) and at rest (KMS); encrypt the on-device database; biometric lock; auto-logout.
• Role-based access, minimum necessary, audit log of every PHI access, patient control over who sees what, ability to revoke a caregiver instantly.
• No PHI in push notification text on the lock screen unless the user opts in; no PHI in logs or analytics.
• Data retention and deletion policy; breach response plan; annual risk assessment; penetration test before pilots.
• Apple and Google store requirements: health-data privacy labels, account deletion in-app, HealthKit/Health Connect usage disclosures.
• Plan for SOC 2 Type II and HITRUST later, as hospitals and insurers will ask.
Regulatory positioning
• Stay in organize-and-remind territory: no diagnosis, no treatment recommendations, no dose calculations. Red-flag alerts only repeat what the patient's own papers say.
• Confirm this positioning with regulatory counsel against FDA's guidance on clinical decision support and general wellness software before adding any feature that interprets data.
• Meal plans are labeled suggestions and are limited to the diet types written in the papers.

Testing
• Unit tests for the schedule generator (tapers, intervals, time zones, daylight saving).
• Extraction regression suite on the golden set in CI.
• Device tests: low battery, Do Not Disturb, app killed, reboot, notification permissions denied.
• Accessibility audit on every release; beta with seniors and caregivers via TestFlight and Play internal testing.
Go-to-market sequence
1.  Free consumer version to prove adherence gains and gather testimonials.
2.  Pilot with one hospital discharge team or home-health agency.
3.  Sell to insurers and health systems using pilot readmission and adherence data; offer branded builds and a dashboard subscription.
Competitive edge to protect: paper-to-plan in one step, with confirm gate, caregiver loop and clinician view in the same product (Medisafe does reminders, MyChart does records).
